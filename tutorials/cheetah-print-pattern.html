How to draw... a cheetah-print pattern

Build one lively cheetah pattern by spacing solid black spots along a loose diagonal flow, then break the gaps with smaller marks before adding natural marker color. Real cheetah markings read as irregular solid spots rather than leopard-style rosettes, and the useful skill is balancing repetition with enough variation to keep the pattern handmade.

Let's draw a cheetah-print pattern

Treat this as one playful practice round: sketch the idea loosely, simplify the shapes, then commit with confident marker outlines and bright fills.

Grab your stuff

Materials

  • PencilWhatever you already have
  • Drawing paperSmooth paper helps marker lines
  • Black felt-tip markerUse the listed fill colors
  • Golden-ochre and warm-tan markersUse the listed fill colors

Use pencil to map every spot before the black marker pass. Let the black fill dry, then sweep golden ochre and warm tan through the remaining paper without covering the intentional highlight gaps.

  1. 01

    Map the pattern flow

    Draw three pale sweeping diagonal guides, then scatter varied center marks for the larger spots across one fixed square crop.

    Marker tip: Ghost each long guide twice, then draw it lightly in one pass. Keep the bands loose and compare the negative spaces between centers so the pattern does not fall into a rigid grid.

  2. 02

    Shape the large solid spots

    Wrap the established centers with uneven round, oval, and slightly angular closed spot shapes, with no rings or open rosette centers.

    Marker tip: Rotate the page and pull each outline in two or three confident arcs. Change the width, tilt, and edge wobble deliberately instead of tracing the same oval repeatedly.

  3. 03

    Fill the open spaces

    Add smaller closed spot shapes in the wide gaps and place a few partial spots where the fixed crop cuts through the pattern edges.

    Marker tip: Squint at the page and target only the largest empty pockets. A few quiet gaps are useful; filling every space evenly will make the print feel mechanical.

  4. 04

    Ink the spot rhythm

    Fill every established large, small, and edge-clipped spot with thick imperfect black felt-tip marker, preserving the hand-drawn edges.

    Marker tip: Let the outline dry briefly, then pull short fill strokes from the edge inward. Turn the page as you work so your hand does not drag through wet black marker.

  5. 05

    Sweep in the cheetah color

    Pull golden-ochre and warm-tan marker strokes around the existing black spots, following the diagonal flow and leaving small paper highlight gaps.

    Marker tip: Work in long directional passes instead of coloring each spot like a separate island. Keep a scrap sheet under your palm and let one band dry before crossing it with another pass.

  6. 06

    Balance the wild pattern

    Strengthen the established black spots, even the existing golden-ochre and warm-tan fills, clean a few negative spaces, and keep the visible marker streaks and paper gaps.

    Marker tip: Scan the pattern corner to corner and strengthen only weak existing marks. Your spot sizes and colors can vary, but adding rosettes, an animal face, or a decorative frame would turn this into a different lesson.
