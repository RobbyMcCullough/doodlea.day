How to draw... a cartoon saxophone with music notes

Swing one face-free cartoon saxophone around a broad bell and rising J-curve, then place seven colored key pads, three rod routes, two music notes, and two sound arcs before adding warm brass marker. This honest archive lesson is a newly made 25-minute study in curved instrument shapes, hardware rhythm, and bold felt-tip overlap—not a claim about earlier visitors, comments, activity, or popularity.

Felt-tip marker mode

Let's draw a cartoon saxophone with music notes

Treat this as one playful practice round: sketch the idea loosely, simplify the shapes, then commit with confident marker outlines and bright fills.

Grab your stuff

Materials

  • PencilWhatever you already have
  • Drawing paperSmooth paper helps marker lines
  • Black felt-tip markerUse the listed fill colors
  • Mustard, ochre, deep-navy, teal, and coral markersUse the listed fill colors

Use pencil for the complete saxophone, seven pad centers, three rod routes, strap loop, two notes, two arcs, highlights, and shadow. Color-code the pads and notes first, let them dry, then ink the established hardware before filling the brass body and navy accents.

  1. 01

    Map the saxophone curve

    Use pale construction to place one flared bell, J-curved body tube, rising neck, mouthpiece, exactly seven pad centers, three rod routes, one strap loop, two note symbols, two sound arcs, three highlight gaps, and one broken shadow footprint.

    Marker tip: Ghost the large body curve before touching down and compare the bell opening with the narrow neck. Count all seven pad centers and reserve the notes, arcs, and highlights now so the final marker pass never invents decoration.

  2. 02

    Shape the brass silhouette

    Wrap the guides with one broad flared bell, curved saxophone body, bent neck, and dark-mouthpiece footprint while preserving every key, note, arc, highlight, loop, and shadow route.

    Marker tip: Pull the bell rim as one confident oval, then connect its sides into the lower bend. Keep the tube thickness steady through the J-curve and let the neck rise without closing the open space above the bell.

  3. 03

    Place the keys and sound marks

    Add exactly seven round key pads, three long key rods, one small strap loop, exactly two music notes, exactly two sound arcs, and three open-paper instrument highlights; color the pads teal and coral and the notes coral.

    Marker tip: Alternate the seven pad colors down the curve and connect each pad to an established rod with a short stem. Let those small fills dry fully before outlining so the black marker stays crisp instead of bleeding into teal and coral.

  4. 04

    Ink the jazzy hardware

    Trace only the established saxophone, seven pads, three rod routes, loop, two notes, two arcs, highlights, and broken shadow with thick handmade black felt-tip, adding short hatching to the existing hardware.

    Marker tip: Ink the small pads and stems before the long body contour, rotating the page as the tube bends. Give the outside silhouette the heaviest line and keep the key rods lighter so the hardware does not flatten the brass shape.

  5. 05

    Fill the brass and accents

    Add mustard and ochre to the established body, deep navy to the mouthpiece and shadow, and leave the already colored keys, notes, and three paper highlights clear with visible marker grain.

    Marker tip: Fill around the key pads with strokes that follow the tube, then layer ochre only on the lower and inside curves. Preserve broad white gaps on the bell and body so the brass reads without a glossy poster finish.

  6. 06

    Tune the final marker rhythm

    Thicken selected established black edges, even the existing fills, deepen the broken shadow, clarify the three highlights, and erase only pale guides that distract.

    Marker tip: Count seven key pads, three rod routes, two notes, and two sound arcs before stopping. Change the brass or accent colors if you like, but keep the same face-free silhouette, hardware rhythm, and handmade marker texture.
