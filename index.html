How to draw... a watering can

Build one cheerful watering can from a low rounded body, then attach a left-leaning spout and a right loop handle. Two falling drops and a simple leaf mark give the 20-minute marker drawing a garden feel while you practice leaving attachment gaps.

20 minIntermediate

Marker ready?

Let's draw a watering can

Treat this as one playful practice round: sketch the idea loosely, simplify the shapes, then commit with confident marker outlines and bright fills.

Grab your stuff

Materials

  • Drawing paperSmooth paper helps marker lines
  • Black felt-tip markerUse the listed fill colors
  • Teal, mustard-yellow, bright-blue, and green markersUse the listed fill colors

Use the black felt tip to establish the body, spout, handle, two drops and leaf before coloring. Let it dry, then fill from the edges inward with broad marker strokes so a little white paper texture stays visible.

  1. 01

    Shape the little can

    Draw a low rounded can body and a narrow oval top opening. Leave short breaks at the upper left and right sides where the spout and handle will join.

    Marker tip: Ghost the top oval twice before inking; the two side breaks keep later attachments from covering a line you already drew.

  2. 02

    Aim the watering spout

    From the left opening, pull two tapered lines up and left. Join them to a tilted oval sprinkler rose and dot its small holes inside the oval.

    Marker tip: Make the rose wider than the spout tip so it reads as a watering head even at card size.

  3. 03

    Loop the carrying handle

    From the right upper break, curve out and down, then return to the lower right body. Add a parallel inner curve to make one thick open loop.

    Marker tip: Rotate the page for the handle arc so both edges stay smooth without mechanical symmetry.

  4. 04

    Drop two splashes

    Under the rose head, draw exactly two separate water teardrops angled down and left. Leave paper around each so they do not touch the spout or can.

    Marker tip: Vary their heights slightly, but keep the two drops large enough to color cleanly.

  5. 05

    Mark the can and ground

    Inside the body, draw one simple leaf and a short center vein. Put a few broken shadow strokes under the can without crossing either water drop.

    Marker tip: Keep the leaf away from the rim and base, then use short horizontal shadow marks to anchor the can.

  6. 06

    Fill the garden colors

    Once the black marker dries, fill the can, spout and handle teal, the rose mustard yellow, both drops bright blue, and the leaf green. Leave narrow white streaks in the marker fills.

    Marker tip: Pull the teal strokes across the rounded body and work from each outline inward. Try a different garden palette next time while keeping the same shapes.
