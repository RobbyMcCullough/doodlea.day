How to draw... a capybara

Build one seated capybara from a low head box and heavy body bean, then wrap exactly two paws around one orange before adding a raised-brow side-eye and bold marker color. This easy 20-minute lesson practices capybara-specific proportions, a fixed fruit overlap, asymmetrical expression, and saturated fills that still show the grain of the felt tip.

Let's draw a capybara

Treat this as one playful practice round: sketch the idea loosely, simplify the shapes, then commit with confident marker outlines and bright fills.

Grab your stuff

Materials

  • PencilWhatever you already have
  • Drawing paperSmooth paper helps marker lines
  • Black felt-tip markerUse the listed fill colors
  • Warm-brown, bright-orange, teal, coral, and deep-navy markersUse the listed fill colors

Use pale pencil for the separate head box and body bean, two ear ticks, two paw routes, orange circle, two foot ovals, face placements, and shadow. Let the black felt tip dry before filling the established warm-brown, orange, teal, coral, and navy regions with directional strokes.

  1. 01

    Map the capybara and orange

    Place one low head box, one separate body bean, two tiny ear ticks, two open paw routes meeting one orange circle, two foot ovals, four face ticks, and a broken shadow route.

    Marker tip: Keep every guide pale and disconnected so the first frame stays copyable in under two minutes.

  2. 02

    Shape the seated capybara

    Connect the long blunt head and heavy seated body, then close exactly two tiny ears and exactly two hind feet while keeping the paw routes open.

    Marker tip: A low muzzle and tiny high-set ears make the silhouette read as a capybara instead of a bear.

  3. 03

    Wrap both paws around the orange

    Turn the two routes into exactly two short forelegs and two paws touching opposite upper sides of the same centered orange.

    Marker tip: Stop the fruit edge behind each paw so the overlap stays clean without erasing keeper lines.

  4. 04

    Give it a raised-brow side-eye

    Add one wide left eye, one half-lidded right eye, one raised right brow, one blunt nose, one crooked closed smile, and exactly six whisker dots.

    Marker tip: Keep the two eyes intentionally different; a symmetric dot-eye smile would lose the character.

  5. 05

    Add leaf fur and highlights

    Add one leaf to the orange, three small fur-tuft groups at the head, cheek, and rump, one body highlight, one fruit highlight, and the broken shadow.

    Marker tip: Use the tufts sparingly so the long muzzle and heavy body remain the main shapes.

  6. 06

    Ink and map the colors

    Ink only established contours and map warm brown, bright orange, teal, coral, and deep navy inside every planned region.

    Marker tip: Pull marker strokes with the body and fruit curves instead of scrubbing across them.

  7. 07

    Pull the marker texture

    Clarify the directional marker streaks, fur direction, exactly four orange dimples, paw accents, highlight edges, and shadow texture.

    Marker tip: Arrange the four dimples as a small diamond and keep that count fixed.

  8. 08

    Finish the cartoon capybara

    Complete the established fills, strengthen selected keeper contours, and clarify the same capybara, orange, expression, tufts, highlights, dimples, and shadow.

    Marker tip: Count two ears, two paws, two feet, six whisker dots, three tuft groups, and four dimples, then stop.
